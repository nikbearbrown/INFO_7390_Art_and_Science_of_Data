Quiz Questions: Understanding Distributions in Financial Returns

Question 1
What is a "return" in finance?
A) The total price of a stock
B) The number of shares you own
C) The percentage change in price over a period
D) The company's profit

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: A return shows how much an investment gained or lost, expressed as a percentage. Option A just shows the price, not the return. Options C and D don’t have anything to do with returns.



Question 2
What shape does a normal distribution have?
A) Flat line
B) Bell curve
C) U-shape
D) Triangle

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The normal distribution is famously known as the “bell curve” because of its symmetrical, bell-like shape. Most values tend to be clustered in the middle, with fewer values at the extremes.



Question 3
What does "skewness" measure?
A) How spread out the data is
B) The average value
C) How symmetric or lopsided the distribution is
D) The highest value

Correct Answer: C

Explanation- Skewness measures asymmetry. Negative skewness means more extreme values on the left (losses), positive skewness means more on the right (gains). Option A describes standard deviation, B is the mean.



Question 4
If a distribution has negative skewness, what does that mean?
A) There are more extreme losses than extreme gains 
B) The average return is negative
C) The data has no outliers
D) The distribution is perfectly symmetric

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Negative skewness indicates a longer tail on the left side, meaning extreme losses occur more frequently than extreme gains. This is common in stock returns.



Question 5
What does "kurtosis" measure?
A) The center of the distribution
B) How fat or thin the tails are
C) The total range of values
D) The most common value

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Kurtosis measures tail thickness. High kurtosis ("fat tails") means extreme events occur more often than a normal distribution predicts.


Question 6
For a normal distribution, what is the excess kurtosis value?
A) -1
B) 0
C) 3
D) 10

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Excess kurtosis for a normal distribution is 0. Values greater than 0 indicate fatter tails than normal (more extreme events).


Question 7
What is a Q-Q plot used for?
A) Showing stock prices over time
B) Predicting future prices
C) Calculating returns
D) Comparing data to a theoretical distribution

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: A Q-Q (quantile-quantile) plot compares your data against a theoretical distribution like the normal. If points follow a straight line, the data matches that distribution.



Question 8
In a Q-Q plot, if the tails curve away from the straight line, what does this indicate?
A) The data is perfectly normal
B) The data has fat tails
C) The data has no variance
D) The data is too small

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: When tails curve away from the diagonal line in a Q-Q plot, it indicates the data has more extreme values than a normal distribution — this is called "fat tails."


Question 9
What does a p-value less than 0.05 mean in a normality test?
A) The data is normal
B) The test failed
C) The data is NOT normal
D) We need more data

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: A p-value < 0.05 means we reject the null hypothesis (that data is normal). This indicates the data does not follow a normal distribution.


Question 10
Which test checks if skewness and kurtosis match a normal distribution?
A) Jarque-Bera test 
B) T-test
C) ANOVA
D) Chi-square test

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: The Jarque-Bera test specifically checks whether a dataset's skewness and kurtosis match those expected from a normal distribution.


Question 11
Why is assuming normality in financial returns dangerous?
A) It makes calculations too easy
B) It requires too much data
C) It overestimates average returns
D) It underestimates the probability of extreme events

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Normal distributions predict extreme events are very rare. In reality, financial crashes happen more often. Assuming normality leads to underestimating risk.


Question 12
What is the formula for a simple return?
A) (Price Today + Price Yesterday) / 2
B) Price Today × Price Yesterday
C) (Price Today - Price Yesterday) / Price Yesterday
D) Price Today / 100

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Simple return is calculated as the price change divided by the original price, giving the percentage gain or loss.


Question 13
Why do analysts often use log returns instead of simple returns?
A) They are easier to calculate
B) They are always positive
C) They have better mathematical properties
D) They eliminate all risk

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Log returns are time-additive (you can sum them over periods) and are more symmetric, making them easier to work with in statistical analysis.


Question 14
What does "fat tails" mean in a distribution?
A) The distribution is very narrow
B) All values are the same
C) The average is very high
D) Extreme events occur more often than expected

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Fat tails mean the probability of extreme events (both positive and negative) is higher than a normal distribution predicts. This is a key feature of financial returns.


Question 15
Based on real data, which statement is TRUE about financial returns?
A) They typically show fat tails and negative skewness
B) They always follow a normal distribution
C) They never have extreme values
D) They are perfectly predictable

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Empirical evidence consistently shows financial returns have fat tails (high kurtosis) and negative skewness (more crashes than booms). This is true across stocks, crypto, and commodities.
